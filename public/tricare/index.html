Getting there

Pyeongtaek to Seoul, handled

Most of our partner clinics are in Gangnam, Seoul. From Camp Humphreys that's about 70 minutes by car, or Line 1 plus a subway transfer.

Transport

  • Saturday group shuttle from the Humphreys gate area on scheduled procedure days
  • Private pickup for procedures where you shouldn't drive yourself home — LASIK, sedation dentistry
  • Or meet us at the clinic if you'd rather drive; we'll send the exact entrance and parking

Bring with you

  • Passport or ID card — clinics are required to record foreign patient identity
  • Your TRICARE card if the visit might turn out to be covered
  • A U.S. or Korean credit card; most clinics take both
  • For orthodontics or implants, any prior X-rays or records you have
Leaving Korea soon? Tell us your PCS date. Orthodontics and multi-stage implant work need a treatment window — we'll tell you honestly if there isn't enough time rather than start something you can't finish.
Questions

Frequently asked

Are you affiliated with TRICARE or the U.S. military?

No. Follow Korea is a private Korean company licensed by the Korean Ministry of Health and Welfare as a foreign-patient facilitator. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by TRICARE, International SOS, the U.S. Department of Defense, or United States Forces Korea. We use the word TRICARE only to describe the insurance program our clients are enrolled in.

What does this cost me?

For elective, self-pay procedures: nothing. We're compensated by the partner clinic, and the price you're quoted is the price you pay — we don't add a markup on top of the clinic's rate. If your procedure total is under ₩1,000,000 there is a ₩40,000 interpreter escort fee covering two hours on site, which we show on the quote before you commit.

For care billed to TRICARE, we charge a flat coordination fee and take no commission from anyone. That's a deliberate legal line, not a preference — see the next question.

Why won't you take a commission on TRICARE-covered care?

TRICARE is a U.S. federal health program, and the federal Anti-Kickback Statute prohibits paying or receiving anything of value in exchange for referring patients whose care that program pays for. Arrangements that ignore this put the clinic and the patient at risk, not just the facilitator. So we keep the two sides of our business strictly separate: commission-based for self-pay elective care, flat-fee for anything touching TRICARE.

Why don't you publish prices on this page?

Korean medical advertising law restricts publishing clinic-identified pricing to the general public. What we can do — and do — is give you an itemized written quote for your specific case, with the clinic named, before you commit to anything.

Will anyone speak English at the clinic?

Some of our partner clinics have English-speaking staff; many don't, and the ones that are best at a given procedure aren't always the ones with the best English. That's why a coordinator goes with you rather than just handing you an address. Interpretation covers the consultation, the consent forms, and payment.

Can I get records for a TRICARE reimbursement claim?

Yes. We ask the clinic for an English itemized invoice and a diagnosis statement with ICD-10 codes, which is what the claim form needs. Ask for this before the visit — it is much harder to get after the fact.

I'm a retiree or veteran living in Korea. Can you help?

Yes for self-pay procedures. If your condition is service-connected, the VA Foreign Medical Program may reimburse care received overseas; that's between you and the VA, but we can supply the documentation.

Is it safe? How do you choose the clinics?

Every partner is a licensed Korean medical institution under a written agreement with us, and we visit them. We are not a review site and we don't pretend to be neutral — we're paid by clinics, which is exactly why we tell you that up front, show you the itemized price before you go, and don't push procedures you didn't ask about.
